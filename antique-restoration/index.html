Preserve the story

Antique furniture restoration in Central Pennsylvania

An heirloom should not emerge looking like an unrelated new object. Restoration balances stability, usability and appearance with the evidence of age that gives the piece meaning.

Begin with what should be preserved

Age alone does not dictate one treatment. An antique may retain a finish worth conserving, carry old repairs that are part of its history or have been altered so many times that a more complete restoration is reasonable. Before removing anything, we discuss the owner’s priorities and look for construction and surface clues.

Sentimental value and market value are not the same. If monetary value is a major concern, seek an independent appraisal before restoration. An appraiser can help document the piece and identify whether intervention could affect collectability.

Restoration may include

  • Re-gluing loose joints and stabilizing frames
  • Repairing drawers, doors and casework
  • Rebuilding damaged or missing wooden elements
  • Reducing scratches, stains and finish disruption
  • Matching surrounding color after repair
  • Refinishing when the existing coating cannot reasonably be preserved

Old surfaces require restraint

Patina, oxidation, tool marks and gentle wear can contribute to the character of old furniture. They should not automatically be sanded away. The objective may be to clean and stabilize, to repair only what has failed, or to restore a more unified appearance. We establish that objective before choosing methods.

Veneer, carvings and delicate elements

Thin veneer and shaped detail allow less margin for aggressive preparation. Lifting veneer, missing edge banding, worn carvings and damaged moldings are evaluated separately from the broad flat surfaces. Close photographs are particularly useful during the initial conversation.

Family furniture can be practical again

Desks, secretaries, tables, cabinets, rockers, chests and bedroom pieces often arrive with a combination of structural and cosmetic problems. Repairing both in the right order can return an heirloom to regular use without pretending it was made yesterday.

Before intervention

Document the piece

Photograph the front, back, underside, hardware, labels or marks, and every damaged area. Share any family history you know.

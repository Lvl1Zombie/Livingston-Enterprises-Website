Furniture refinishing and repair questions

Every piece is different, but these answers explain the decisions that most often affect scope, expectations and cost.

How much does furniture refinishing cost?

Size is only one factor. Construction, existing coatings, carvings or profiles, structural repair, desired color and protective finish all affect labor. Photographs can start the conversation, but a reliable price generally requires enough information to understand the full scope.

How long will my project take?

Repair, stripping, surface preparation, color development, multiple coats and curing all require time. The active workshop schedule matters too. Livingston provides a realistic project window after assessment rather than promising the same turnaround for a chair and a full dining set.

Is my furniture worth refinishing?

Solid wood, good joinery, meaningful history and the cost of a comparable replacement are good reasons to investigate restoration. Particleboard, badly compromised veneer and some factory finishes may limit what is practical. We will say so when replacement or leaving the piece alone makes more sense.

Can veneered furniture be refinished?

Often, but not automatically. Veneer is a thin layer of real wood and can be sanded through. Its thickness, adhesion, missing areas and previous work determine which preparation methods are safe.

Can you match the original color or another piece?

We can work toward an original appearance or coordinate with another piece using preparation, stain and layered color. Wood species, grain, sun exposure and age influence the result, so “matching” is usually an informed visual target rather than a paint-code guarantee.

Do you make repairs without refinishing the entire piece?

Yes, when the construction and finish permit it. A repair may still disturb color or coating immediately around a joint or replacement part. We explain whether that area can be blended or whether a broader surface would look more consistent.

Can scratches, water rings and heat marks be repaired?

Some can be reduced locally; others show that the coating has failed across a larger area. Damage that reaches the wood requires a different approach than a mark contained in the clear finish. Clear close-up photographs help identify the likely scope.

Will refinishing reduce an antique’s value?

It can for some collectible furniture. If market value is a major concern, obtain an independent appraisal before intervention. An appraiser can help determine whether conservation, limited repair or restoration is appropriate.

Do you offer pickup and delivery?

Pickup and delivery are discussed according to dimensions, customer location and scheduling. Include the piece’s approximate size and your town when requesting an assessment.

What photographs should I send?

Send the complete piece from the front, broad finished surfaces, every damaged area, and the back or underside when practical. Include labels, maker’s marks and hardware on antique pieces. Good photographs reduce assumptions during the first conversation.
